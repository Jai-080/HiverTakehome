# Decision Log

Final, submission-ready. Each entry is a decision with a stated reason and, where relevant, the number that justified it — not a narration of what happened.

- **Dropped the raw `customer_tweet`/`brand_reply` columns from the two committed CSVs, forward-fix not history rewrite.** A pre-submission review caught that `amazonhelp_working.csv` (9,000 rows) and `amazonhelp_heldout.csv` (4,000 rows) still carried the *raw* text columns alongside the PII-redacted `_clean` ones — the redaction in `clean_and_subsample.py` only ever touched `customer_tweet_clean`/`brand_reply_clean`, never removed the originals it was derived from. Verified with the exact same regex from `clean_and_subsample.py`: raw columns matched the phone pattern 420+7 times (working) and 182+5 times (heldout); the `_clean` columns matched 0 times in both, confirming the redaction step itself was correct — the raw columns just should never have been committed alongside it. Confirmed via `grep` across every script in `src/` that nothing downstream reads the raw columns (only `_clean`/`_id`) before dropping them. Fixed as a new commit on top of current history rather than rewriting/force-pushing: the underlying tweets are already public data from the published Kaggle dataset, not a secret leak, so a forward-fix is the right tradeoff against the time cost and risk of rewriting history this close to submission.

- **Brand: AmazonHelp** — largest single-brand slice (169,840 replies vs. AppleSupport's 106,860, of 2.81M rows), confirmed from data, not assumed. Its dominant deflection pattern is a link redirect (~41.3% of replies), not "DM us" phrasing (~0.5%) — a substance filter built around DM language alone would have massively undercounted boilerplate, so `has_link` became the actual filter driving what enters the RAG grounding corpus.


- **Pivoted the entire pipeline off Gemini to local models** after two free-tier quota walls: `embed_content` capped at 1,000/day, exhausted after 1,001 calls across two runs; `generate_content` capped at 20 requests/day / 5 per minute on this project, confirmed via the AI Studio dashboard (not assumed) before committing more budget. Replaced with `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5` (all embeddings) and `microsoft/Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct` (4-bit, GPU via a corrected CUDA torch build) for classification, drafting, and judging — zero per-request cost or rate ceiling from that point on.

- **PII redaction extended in two stages.** Phone/email regex on the working set first (`<PHONE>`/`<EMAIL>`, 6,217 customer-tweet and 76 brand-reply rows affected). After finding a different customer's name and agent initials leaking into drafted replies, extended the same redaction discipline to RAG few-shot examples (`clean_retrieved_reply()`) — a distinct identity-leakage problem from the account-specific-facts guard, which was already holding correctly.

- **First-touch filter: 49.9% of reconstructed pairs (84,177 of 168,814) were follow-up turns, not conversation-openers** — excluded before dedup/subsampling, dropping the cleaned population to 55,727 rows. Matters because a taxonomy or RAG corpus built on fragments like "yes it did" / "thanks" would ground on responses to context absent from the tweet being classified.

- **Reply-template cap (5/template) barely fired — 3,862 of 113,014 rows (~3.4%)** — because AmazonHelp replies are overwhelmingly non-templated at the exact-string level (103,384 unique templates). Kept as protection against canned-response inflation in the RAG corpus regardless; the low hit rate is itself the finding — fuzzy near-duplicate detection would matter more here than exact-match dedup.

- **Intent taxonomy cross-validated via two independent clustering runs** (Gemini embeddings then local bge-large, different sample sizes) rather than one — 8 classifier intents plus 1 keyword-based fraud/security override that sits outside the classifier entirely (too rare to cluster reliably). `marketplace_seller_trust` is the one weak spot: clear in the first run, not reproduced in the second — kept anyway since it's operationally distinct (seller-trust routing, not standard refund flow), flagged as thin, single-run evidence.

- **Weakly-supervised pseudo-labels (700 rows, LLM-classified) trained the simple baseline before any hand-labels existed** — the golden set didn't exist until Hour 6.5-8.5, so this was the only path to a trained baseline early enough to matter. Used the *unbalanced* classifier, not the class-weight-balanced refit, as "the simple baseline" deliberately: its known minority-class collapse (3 of 8 intents at 0% recall) isn't a bug to fix, it's what a naive baseline is supposed to look like next to the real system.

- **RAG corpus substance-filtered (has_link==False AND reply length >=40 chars) down to 4,228 rows**, then retrieved as top-20 similarity → filter to predicted intent → top-3, falling back to pure similarity when fewer than 3 of the top-20 match intent (expected for sparse intents like marketplace_seller_trust). Fraud-flagged messages skip retrieval and generation entirely — a fixed holding message, no LLM call — since a security signal shouldn't be routed through a reply-drafting model at all.

- **Name leakage in draft_reply.py needed a policy fix, not more regex.** Two rounds of pattern-matching (greeting-prefix template, then mid-sentence comma-name template) each caught only the case just observed and missed the next variant. The fix that actually worked was banning any person's name categorically in the generation prompt, once a fabricated name ("Alex," present in none of the 3 retrieved examples) proved the model could invent leaks no amount of *input* cleaning would catch.

- **Escalation policy: three-tier design** — fraud override (hard trigger) → always-escalate on 4 high-risk intents regardless of confidence → validate_draft failures force escalation → confidence+similarity gate for everything else. Both gate thresholds (confidence ≥0.90, retrieval similarity ≥0.74) are the 25th percentile of their actual observed score distributions, not guessed defaults — confidence in particular is heavily clustered near the top (median = 25th percentile = 0.90), so there was no natural slope to pick from otherwise.

- **Golden set: 200 rows — 160 stratified across the 8 intents + 40 oversampled hard cases** (fraud/scam/police keywords, near-verbatim retrieval stress >0.9 similarity, "pay/paid for X" vocabulary-overlap stress, low retrieval similarity), deliberately non-representative so the eval step can report both an unbiased stratified-only number and a hard-case-inflated headline number. `near_verbatim_stress` hit its natural ceiling at 3 of a 10 target (only 3 such rows exist in the entire 4,000-row held-out pool) — backfilled the shortfall from `low_retrieval_similarity` and disclosed the true count rather than lowering the threshold or padding silently.

- **Judge shares a model with the generator (Phi-3-mini) — no second local model fit the budget/hardware.** Mitigated via a distinctly-worded judge persona and different decoding (temperature=0.3 sampling vs. the generator's greedy decoding), but calibration against 40 hand-scored rows shows the risk is live, not just theoretical: 4 of 5 rubric dimensions correlate significantly with human scores (rho 0.37-0.45, p<0.05), but `tone_match` — the dimension most sensitive to a model favoring its own style — doesn't (rho=-0.08, p=0.63), despite the highest raw agreement (87.5% exact-or-adjacent) of any dimension.

- **Trivial baseline's majority-class intent and "escalate everything" decision are both drawn from the training distribution, never from golden-set labels** — using the golden set's own label distribution to pick a majority class would leak test information into a baseline meant to represent doing nothing clever. "Escalate everything" was chosen over "auto everything" as the fixed rule because it's the safer default for a support system and gives a real precision/recall floor to measure the real policy's over-escalation against.

- **Raw dataset (493MB, 2.8M rows) and all derived CSVs are gitignored — only source code and reports are committed.** Reproduction depends on the user supplying their own copy of the Kaggle Customer Support on Twitter dataset and re-running the pipeline from `load_and_eda.py` forward; nothing in the repo is a self-contained data snapshot. README instructions make this handoff explicit rather than assuming the data is just there.
